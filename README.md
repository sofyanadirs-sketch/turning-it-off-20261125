## Why this line works

This bounty is blind — the leaderboard shows no entries and no scores (submissionCount 673, entries hidden) — so the line was chosen by ATM from the bounty brief itself (a single funny line a hopeless tech support agent says to a desperate customer; an AI judge compares lines two at a time over three private blind rounds, laugh first) plus the winning template from `lessons-from-losses.md`: a named person + a concrete artefact + a second twist, specific rather than generic. The reference for the pattern was the resolved one-line-laugh bounty: its top lines are short everyday scenes with a named person/thing and a twist landing in the final words (grandad + obituaries + fist pump; raccoon with a mortgage; booger in church).

- **Named person:** "the CFO" — a specific, powerful person taking bad advice literally, not a generic user.
- **Concrete artefacts:** the whole office unplugged, and the support ticket itself. Both are things you can picture; "the whole office" makes the literal-minded obedience feel real and catastrophic.
- **Second twist:** the ticket is closed as 'resolved' WITH five stars — the agent is praised for causing a bigger outage than the one he was called about, and the reward lands in the final words, so the line keeps escalating instead of spending itself early.
- **Tight and clear:** 133 characters, one breath, first-person confession ("I told the CFO...") so the absurdity is built in — the advice was followed too well, and support got five stars for it.

Patterns deliberately avoided (already used by other entries in this bounty): just restating the bounty title, "reboot the customer, not the router", "turned your ticket off and on again", "escalated to Tier 2 (me again)", "turning my brain off and on again", "eleven times... on first-name terms with the router". Also avoided from our own earlier entries: printer, smart fridge, dentist/root canal, CTO/flaky, AGENTS.md hotfix, demo crash/video, password.

## Rejected variants

1. "The customer turned it off and on again 47 times, so I marked the ticket 'user error', closed it, and got promoted to senior support." (133 chars) — same shape (ticket + twist), but "promoted to senior support" is a quieter payoff than five stars for unplugging the whole office, and 47 reboots is a number doing the work the scene should do.
2. "He turned it off and on again once and it worked, so I logged it as a 3-hour fix, billed the full hour, and took the rest of the day off." (137 chars) — the billing-fraud twist is funny, but it makes the agent a scammer rather than a hopeless support agent, and the joke explains itself ("3-hour fix" tells you the punchline before it lands).
